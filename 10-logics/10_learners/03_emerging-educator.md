# The Emerging Educator

## 3 Learner Archetypes

**Note:** Learner archetypes = research-informed hypotheses developed from literature research and instructional design principles; they provide an initial framework for designing the UnitEd MVP, but should be validated and refined through user interviews and field research.

**UnitEd's MVP prioritizes the Emerging Educator archetype.** Designing for three distinct archetypes would introduce variability that conflicts with focused MVP development. Additionally, Emerging Educators represent the learner group most likely to benefit from structured PD and directly address UnitEd's mission of improving teaching quality among educators already in the classroom—a key lever in tackling the global teacher shortage.

### The Emerging Educator

*Local educator who regularly teaches but has limited or no formal pedagogical training*

Examples: often contract, para-teachers or community teachers — [Contract Teachers in Sub-Saharan Africa](https://teachertaskforce.org/sites/default/files/2020-07/Contract%20teachers%20in%20sub-Saharan%20Africa_EN.pdf) | [Community Teachers - Luminos Fund](https://luminosfund.org/luminos-method/community-teachers/)

#### Design Implications

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Defining Characteristic** | Local educator who regularly teaches a class or group of learners but has limited or no formal pedagogical training |
| **Primary Learning Need** | Develop strong pedagogical foundations to improve classroom practice and learner outcomes. |
| **Technical Considerations** | Smartphone or shared computer; moderate digital confidence; downloadable and printable resources are important; learning often happens alongside teaching responsibilities. |
| **Priority Content** | Pedagogical foundations, classroom management, lesson planning, assessment, active learning, inclusive teaching, teaching in context. |
| **Design Priorities for UnitEd** | Scaffolded learning pathways, modular courses, practical examples, lesson templates, printable job aids, reflection activities, immediate classroom application. |

####

#### Detailed Archetype Profile

*Local educator who regularly teaches but has limited or no formal pedagogical training*

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Teaching Responsibility** | Plans, delivers, and manages regular teaching for a class or learning group. |
| **Pedagogical Preparation** | Limited or no formal teacher education. Teaching experience has largely been acquired through practice and in-service learning. |
| **Motivation & Support** | Committed to becoming a more effective educator and improving learner outcomes. Access to professional development and mentoring is often limited. |
| **Time & Commitment** | Long-term teaching commitment but limited protected time for professional learning. |
| **Digital Access & Literacy** | Variable depending on context; often relies on smartphones or shared devices with intermittent internet access. |
| **Primary Learning Need** | Classroom management, lesson planning, pedagogy, assessment, inclusive teaching, and practical strategies for effective teaching in low-resource settings. |
| **Challenges & Barriers** | Limited access to formal professional development, mentoring, and high-quality teaching resources. Faces large or mixed-age classes, resource constraints; challenge of translating experience into sound teaching practice. |
